A personal attention assistant, built on TypeSafe Jev

Attention, pulled left and right

Deep work needs a couple of unbroken hours. Our tools hand them out one ping at a time.

  • Slack, email, CI, the calendar, family: every app gets to tap you on the shoulder, and every tap costs you the thread you were holding.
  • Most of those taps could have waited. They got through because Sami put URGENT in the subject line.
  • So the focus time you blocked in your calendar is the first thing to go.

URGENT is a word, not a deadline

When every ping looks the same, the wording decides what feels urgent. Loud beats important.

14:05 · Layla, CEO
“URGENT!!! Can we make the investor deck background blue instead of grey?? Need it ASAP!!!”
Feels like a fire. It's a colour change that could wait until 16:00.
14:20 · Rami, engineer
“hey, is checkout supposed to show 0 JOD for some users?”
Feels like a question. It's a pricing bug the day before the demo.

Today there are two bad options

Notifications on
Everything gets through. The focus block lasts five minutes, and the real bug sits under the shouting.
Do not disturb
Nothing gets through. Not the error-rate spike after the deploy, and not the school calling about his daughter.

What he needs is someone at the door who knows him and his day, and protects the focus block.

Doorman stands at the door

  • Interrupted you only when it needs him and can't wait: the 0 JOD bug, the customer launching today, the school.
  • Held for later when it can wait, and the sender gets a kind auto-reply saying when he'll be back.
  • Forwarded when someone else can handle it, like CI machinery failures going to DevOps.
  • Needs your call when it honestly can't tell. “got a sec?” from the cofounder gets a question, not a guess.
  • Family always reaches him, unless the message can clearly wait. A “pay the school fees next month” becomes a reminder in his calendar, with clashes flagged.

It asks what a good assistant would ask

  1. Jev answers small questions about each message, with honest probabilities: Does this need Sanad himself? Can it wait a few hours? Is the urgency only in the wording? Does it block the release? Is it family?
  2. Plain rules decide what to do with those answers. You can read them, test them, and change them on the Rules page: the daily interruption budget, quiet hours, who counts as family or VIP.
  3. The LLM only writes the words: auto-replies, CI explanations, the evening summary. It never decides who gets through.

Deadlines are read by code, not guessed by a model: a deadline in the next two hours always gets through.

What we're honest about

  • Personal sources (Gmail, Calendar) are opt-in; nothing leaves the laptop except the model calls.
  • All people and data are fictional. One day, 34 hand-labelled events, 8 held out: a small set.
  • Labels and rules were corrected after the first real run; every change is logged in data/CHANGELOG.md.
  • The LLM comparison answers the same questions in one prompt at default reasoning; a better prompt might do better.
  • The calendar connection is demo data in Google Calendar's format; nothing is written to a real account yet.
